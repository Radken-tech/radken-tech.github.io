Network Upgrade Proposal

Prepared for Mwangaza Academy, a two-campus private primary school in Dar es Salaam

Prepared byKenny Rodgers, Radken Tech
SitesMain campus (3 blocks) and Annex (1 block), 450 m apart
Users520 pupils · 38 staff · ~160 devices

1Executive summary

Mwangaza Academy's network has grown one router at a time. Today it runs on a single 20 Mbps fibre link with no backup, consumer routers in each block, and one shared Wi-Fi password used by staff, pupils and visitors alike. The result is slow internet at peak times, Wi-Fi dead spots in two classroom blocks, and no protection for the school's records if the link or a router fails.

This proposal sets out a phased upgrade that gives every block reliable wired and wireless coverage, links the Annex to the main campus, separates staff, pupil and guest traffic, and adds a backup internet line and power protection.

Indicative budget: TZS 18–29 million for equipment and installation, which can be spread across three school terms. All figures are estimates, to be confirmed by supplier quotations.

2Current situation

From the school's requirements form and the equipment list provided:

  • Internet: one 20 Mbps fibre line at the main campus. The Annex shares it over a long outdoor cable that fails in heavy rain.
  • Equipment: 4 consumer Wi-Fi routers and 2 unmanaged 8-port switches, mostly over 5 years old.
  • Cabling: mixed Cat5 runs along walls with no labelling and no central cabinet.
  • Uses: school management system, staff email, a 30-seat computer lab, online exam practice and 6 CCTV cameras.
  • Power: frequent short cuts; a generator covers the office block only.

4Proposed solution

4.1 Internet and backup link

Keep the current fibre as the main line and add a 4G/5G backup router. A firewall switches over automatically, so the school stays online when the fibre drops.

4.2 Core and access switching

Install a lockable wall cabinet in the main office with a managed core switch, and a PoE access switch in each block. PoE powers the Wi-Fi points and cameras through the network cable, so no extra power sockets are needed.

4.3 Wi-Fi coverage

Replace the consumer routers with 12 ceiling-mounted access points, all managed from one place: 9 at the main campus, including the staff room and hall, and 3 at the Annex.

4.4 Separate networks for each group

  • Staff: full access to the school system and printers.
  • Pupils and lab: internet only, with unsafe sites filtered.
  • Guests: internet only, speed-limited and isolated.
  • CCTV: cameras kept on their own network.

4.5 Linking the Annex

Replace the outdoor cable with a point-to-point wireless link between the two campuses, which is unaffected by rain or digging.

4.6 Power protection

Add a UPS in the main cabinet and smaller UPS units at each block switch, giving about 30–60 minutes of runtime during power cuts.

6Implementation phases

Phase 1 · Term 1Firewall, backup internet, core cabinet and UPS. This fixes the highest risks first.
Phase 2 · Term 2Cabling, block switches and Wi-Fi for the main campus.
Phase 3 · Term 3Annex wireless link, Annex Wi-Fi, and the final network separation.

Work is scheduled for weekends and school holidays, so lessons are not disrupted.

7Maintenance and support

  • Monthly check of firewall logs, backup-line status and UPS batteries by the school's chosen technician.
  • Change Wi-Fi passwords each term; keep the guest network separate at all times.
  • Keep equipment firmware up to date, and record every device in the school's IT asset register.

8Next steps

  1. Board reviews this proposal and approves Phase 1.
  2. The school sends the bill of quantities to 2–3 suppliers for quotations.
  3. Radken Tech helps compare the quotations against the specification, if requested.
